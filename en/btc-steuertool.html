Calculate Bitcoin Taxes Yourself: FiFo, Holding Period & the Open-Source Tool for Your Tax Return

by Alien Investor

You hold Bitcoin in self-custody. Good. You buy through multiple brokers. Even better. And now the tax authority asks which coins you bought when, which of them are tax-free and exactly how high your taxable gain is.

For exactly this problem there is the BTC Tax Tool — an open-source tool I developed myself for my own tax return (CLI, web version and app). It reads your CSV exports from BitBox, 21bitcoin, Bison, Swissquote, Strike, Pocket and Bisq and automatically calculates what has to go to the tax office and what remains tax-free.

1. The Foundation: What German Tax Law Says

Bitcoin is classified in Germany as a private disposal transaction (§ 23 EStG). The rules:

Situation Tax treatment
BTC held for one year or less, then sold Profit is a taxable event — tax is due once the exemption threshold is reached
BTC held for more than one year, then sold Profit tax-free
Transfer between your own wallets/accounts The transferred holdings: not a taxable event
Network/withdrawal fee paid in bitcoin Disposal of the fee portion at the daily rate (BMF circular of 6 March 2025, paras. 54, 60) — usually cents, the tool includes them
Bitcoin given away or donated Not a disposal, but the coins leave your holdings — acquisition date and cost carry over to the recipient (Sec. 23 (1) sentence 3 EStG)
Profit under 600 EUR/year (until 2023) Exemption threshold — not a single cent in tax
Profit under 1,000 EUR/year (from 2024) Exemption threshold — not a single cent in tax

The method is called FiFo — First In, First Out. That means: what you buy first, you also sell first. Sounds simple, but gets complicated quickly when you buy from five brokers and your BTC moves between multiple wallets. On top of that, the German Federal Ministry of Finance circular of 6 March 2025 requires in para. 62 a wallet-based view — FiFo applies per wallet, not across everything at once. Since October 2026 the tool calculates exactly that way and also treats every exchange account as a wallet of its own.

The Exemption Threshold Is a Trap

Many people think: "I'm under 1,000 EUR, so it's irrelevant." Wrong. The exemption threshold is not a tax allowance. If your gain reaches or exceeds it, the entire taxable profit becomes taxable — not just the part above it. A profit of 1,001 EUR is therefore fully taxable — not just that one euro.

2. The Problem with Self-Custody

Tax apps like CoinTracking or Blockpit work well for exchanges that record everything. For genuine self-custody holders with hardware wallets, problems arise:

  • You transfer BTC from your BitBox to an exchange for a sale — this looks like a purchase.
  • You have multiple wallets — the cost basis travels with them.
  • Swissquote buys in USD — the historical exchange rate must be correctly factored in.
  • You enter your complete financial data into a cloud app — that contradicts the whole point of self-custody.

The BTC Tax Tool runs locally on your machine. No cloud, no registration, no third-party servers. Your data stays with you.

noKYC Purchases and Private Sales

Anyone who buys BTC without a KYC broker — via Robosats, HodlHodl, directly person-to-person or for cash — has no broker CSV (the tool reads Bisq exports directly, see the table above). For this there is manual_buys.csv: a simple text file in the project folder where you enter each purchase with date, BTC amount and EUR amount. 

date,btc_amount,eur_amount,note,kyc
2024-07-22,0.00500000,280.00,Robosats trade,
2024-11-05,0.00250000,148.50,P2P cash,
2024-12-01,0.00200000,190.00,Coinbase purchase (broker without parser),ja

The kyc column is optional. Empty means noKYC (default). With ja you mark a purchase from a broker that has no import of its own yet: it then counts towards the KYC holdings and appears in the report for the tax office.

Same principle for private sales without a broker: manual_sales.csv with the same first four columns. The optional fifth column is called no_kyc there and works the other way round: ja = sale from the noKYC holdings, internal report only. Empty = normal sale, appears in the report for the tax office. The tool reads both files automatically and processes them like any other purchase or sale.

On the legal situation: A tax report with correct acquisition costs is usually enough. You do not have to hand over wallet addresses unprompted. In individual cases, however, the tax office can request addresses, transaction hashes and holdings (BMF letter of 06.03.2025, para. 101, 104). So keep your records, including for P2P purchases.

⚡ Update October 2026: FiFo per Wallet — as the BMF Circular Requires

The German Federal Ministry of Finance circular of 6 March 2025 states in para. 62: a wallet-based view applies. So FiFo does not run across all holdings together, but per wallet. Until now the tool calculated one pooled holding across all sources. Since version 1.2 every BitBox wallet is a FiFo holding of its own, and the tool treats every broker account as a wallet of its own as well (paras. 20 and 90 name the trading platform as a separate unit) — the only method used in the official documents, no toggle.

  • Transfers carry their history: on a transfer between your own wallets, the sending wallet gives up its oldest units; purchase date and cost travel into the receiving wallet and are sorted in there by purchase date. The transfer itself remains a non-disposal.
  • Automatic matching: outgoing and incoming transfers are linked by transaction ID, otherwise by amount (with or without fee) and time within 48 hours. Ambiguous cases are not linked but reported; special cases such as pooled withdrawals are solved with the optional file transfer_zuordnung.csv.
  • Nothing silently becomes a sale: an outgoing transfer without a matching incoming one counts as a transfer to one of your wallets that was not loaded, and is reported. So: load the exports of all your wallets.
  • The reports explain the method: tax report and tax evidence state the method as FiFo per wallet under paras. 61 f. and list the transfers between your own wallets; the tax report additionally shows the holdings per wallet at year end. Your BitBox wallets appear there only as "BitBox-Wallet", with several wallets "BitBox-Wallet 1", "BitBox-Wallet 2" … — file names and real labels appear solely in the new internal file wallet_abgleich_intern_YEAR.txt, together with the comparison to the former pooled calculation.
  • What may change: depending on which units sat in which wallet, a sale may now be matched to a different purchase than before — with a different holding period and gain. With my own data the differences were single-digit euro amounts per year; yours may look different. The internal comparison file shows the difference per year.

How acquisition data travels on a transfer is not spelled out in the BMF circular. The tool chooses the obvious reading — the sending wallet gives up units by FiFo, purchase date and cost are preserved — and discloses it in the evidence document. No tax advice.

⚡ Update August 2026: Fees Paid in Bitcoin and Gifts Are Now Included

Until now the tool treated transfers between your own wallets as tax-neutral, full stop. That is true for the transferred holdings — but not for the network fee you pay in bitcoin along the way. You hand it over in exchange for a service, and under the logic of the German Federal Ministry of Finance circular of 6 March 2025 (paras. 33, 54, 60) that is a small disposal of the fee portion. The tool now books it — which also means the reported holdings finally match your wallet.

  • Fees in BTC: miner fees on BitBox sends (including wallet-internal ones), 21bitcoin withdrawal fees, Bisq trading fees — each is matched to a lot via FiFo, valued at the daily closing price and listed with gain/loss in the report (section "GEBÜHREN IN BITCOIN"). The gain counts towards the exemption threshold. In practice this amounts to cents to a few euros per year.
  • Prices offline: the daily closing prices (Bitstamp BTC/EUR since 2017) ship as a table inside the tool. No price service is queried — your transaction data still never leaves the browser, and a report generated tomorrow is identical to one generated today.
  • Gifts and donations: put a word like "Spende", "Geschenk", "donation" or "gift" into the transaction note in the BitBox app. The tool then books it as a gratuitous transfer: no disposal, no gain — but the coins leave your holdings, and the report shows purchase date and cost basis per lot, because both carry over to the recipient (Sec. 23 (1) sentence 3 EStG).
  • Transparency: the tax evidence document explains the method itself, citing the paragraph numbers. Anyone who prefers a different treatment of the fees can see exactly which lines are affected.

Why no tax-advisor opinion on this? Because there is no explicit administrative statement and no court ruling — only the logic of the BMF circular and the practice of the major crypto tax tools, which do the same. The tool picks the traceable, documented method and lays it open. This is not tax advice.

⚡ Update June 2026: Bisq Direct Import + Strict noKYC Separation

The tool now supports direct import of the Bisq trade history CSV. Simply place the export from Bisq Classic ("Portfolio → History → Export to CSV", with Bisq set to German, as the parser reads the German column headers) as bisq.csv in the Broker/ folder — the tool automatically recognises all completed purchases.

  • Bisq direct import: No more manual entry. The parser reads price, BTC amount, EUR amount and fees directly from the Bisq export.
  • Dedicated noKYC file: noKYC purchases do not appear in any tax-office document. They go into a separate file nokyc_intern_YEAR.txt — with a clear warning header at the top so it can never accidentally be handed in along with the official documents.
  • Separate FiFo pools: KYC and noKYC holdings live in two strictly separated FiFo pools. A broker sale can never be matched against a Bisq purchase as its cost basis — the official proof document is guaranteed to stay internally consistent.
  • noKYC wallets: Running a dedicated wallet just for noKYC sats? Place its CSV in the subfolder bitbox/nokyc/ — all movements appear exclusively in the internal report.
  • noKYC sales: manual_sales.csv now has an optional no_kyc column. P2P sales from your noKYC holdings are calculated internally with FiFo matching, holding period and gain — but only ever appear in the internal file.

Also: the holding period is now calculated calendar-exact (anniversary date instead of a rigid 365-day count) — correct across leap years too.

For Robosats, HodlHodl and other P2P purchases without a CSV export, manual_buys.csv remains the right approach — also with noKYC separation.

Bisq is currently the only noKYC platform with a dedicated parser. The tool is MIT-licensed — anyone wanting to contribute an importer for Robosats, HodlHodl or other platforms is very welcome.

5. Your First Report (CLI)

Output a report for a specific year: 

.venv/bin/python src/main.py --year 2024

All years at once: 

.venv/bin/python src/main.py --all

With formal documentation for your accountant or tax office: 

.venv/bin/python src/main.py --year 2024 --csv --nachweis

The result lands in reports/: a readable tax report, a print-ready formal proof of calculation and optionally CSV tables for Excel.

6. What You Submit to the Tax Office (Web, App & CLI)

The proof alone is not enough. The tax office wants to know that the purchase data is genuine. So always submit two parts:

  • The tax proof — what the tool generates (steuernachweis_YEAR.txt).
  • The original broker CSVs — as evidence that the purchases actually took place.

The tool shows in the proof under "Source" exactly which broker provided which lot — so you can trace every single sale without gaps.

7. What This Tool Is Not

Not a replacement for a tax advisor. If you have taxable gains, have the results reviewed — especially in the first year. The tool calculates correctly according to FiFo, but you are responsible for the accuracy of your inputs.

What it is: a tool that informs and prepares you — instead of leaving you blindly trusting a cloud app you don't control.

As an app: Android and Linux desktop

Since version 1.0 (2 October 2026) the tax tool is also available as an app for Android (GrapheneOS) and as a Flatpak for the Linux desktop — free and open source like everything else. Interface and calculation core are the same as in the web version, with one difference: after installation the app downloads nothing. Pyodide, the calculation core, the ECB exchange rates and the BTC daily closing prices are built in. The reports are byte-identical to those of the CLI tool.

  • Android: no internet permission (the only permission in the manifest is the AndroidX-generated signature permission of the app itself, which grants nothing), screenshots and app switcher blocked (FLAG_SECURE), no backup of app data. You pick CSVs and ZIPs with the file picker and save reports through the Android save dialog.
  • Linux desktop: Flatpak without network permission and without file-system access — files come in through the file dialog or drag & drop and go out through the save dialog.
  • In-app guide behind the “?” button (German and English). The reports stay in German, they are meant for the German tax office.

Limits, stated honestly

  • Internal review, not an independent audit. Before the release an internal security audit covered the app shells and the latest changes to the calculation core; all findings were fixed before publication.
  • No screenshot protection on the desktop — only the Android app blocks screenshots.
  • Rate tables have a cut-off date. If a purchase or sale in USD/CHF lies after the end of the built-in ECB table, the calculation stops with an explanation instead of using an old rate. For fees paid in bitcoin after the end of the BTC price table (as of v1.2: 14 August 2026) the report marks the gain as “not determined” and warns. New rates come with an app update, at the latest in January for the past year.
  • No tax advice. The app calculates; responsibility for the tax return stays with you.
