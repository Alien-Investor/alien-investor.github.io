Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN)
Why the P/E of 21 Is Misleading

by Alien Investor – November 8, 2026, after the third-quarter results (replaces the November 2025 analysis)

A year ago, Amazon stock traded at almost 33 times earnings. Today the price is 11% higher and the P/E ratio is 21. At first glance, Amazon suddenly looks cheap. The second glance shows where the jump in earnings comes from: about 40% of pre-tax income over the last twelve months did not come from retail, advertising or the cloud, but mainly from the revaluation of the stake in Anthropic. At the same time, Amazon is building data centers at a pace that consumes more cash than the business brings in. Free cash flow has fallen to −$42B, and long-term debt has more than doubled since the start of the year. This analysis replaces the November 2025 version and separates what is business from what is accounting.

“A profit you cannot spend is a valuation. A data center you have paid for is a bet.”

Three developments shape the year:

  • AWS is accelerating: In the third quarter of 2025 the cloud grew 20%, a year later 39%. The backlog stands at $578B.
  • Capex at a new level: Over the last twelve months, a net $210.7B went into data centers, chips, logistics and satellites, 82% more than a year earlier. For 2026 Amazon expects about $220B, for 2027 “meaningfully more”.
  • Gains on investments: In the first half, Amazon booked about $70B of pre-tax gains, mainly from Anthropic. No cash came in. These gains push the P/E down to 21.

The weak growth in retail is partly a calendar effect. In 2026, Prime Day fell in the second quarter for most large countries; in 2025 it was entirely in the third. Amazon says advertising was affected too: excluding currency effects it grew 12% in the third quarter, after 26% in the second. For the fourth quarter Amazon again expects growth “in the high teens”.

Revenue grew a little over 11% per year from 2021 to 2025 [own calculation]. The real story is the margin: in 2022, 2.4% of revenue remained as operating income; over the last twelve months it was 13.1%. That is mainly due to AWS, whose margin of 38.7% in the third quarter is a multiple of the retail segments’, and to the growth of advertising and seller fees, whose margins Amazon does not disclose separately.

Earnings per share follow this trend only roughly, because other income has been bending them in both directions for years. In 2021 the stake in electric-vehicle maker Rivian brought a valuation gain of $11.8B, in 2022 a loss of $12.7B. The 2022 loss came from the Rivian stake; operating income stayed positive at $12.2B. In 2026 it is Anthropic, and the effect is many times larger: over the last twelve months $69.5B came from other income, more than the entire operating income of 2024 ($68.6B).

The third quarter in detail: Operating income rose to $28.1B from $17.4B. The comparison is distorted. The prior-year quarter carried $2.5B for the FTC settlement in the Prime case and about $1.8B in severance; without both it would have been $21.7B. Net income, by contrast, dipped slightly to $20.7B (prior year $21.2B), because back then it contained a $9.5B pre-tax gain from Anthropic, this time other income of −$0.7B. Amazon calls its 13.9% operating margin the highest in its history.

About the data: P/E, P/B, equity ratio and return on equity in the screenshot match the quarterly report as of September 30, 2026. Four values need an explanation. The tool’s free cash flow of −$46.9B is operating cash flow minus gross capex. Amazon nets $4.5B of proceeds and incentives against capex and arrives at −$42.4B. The debt/equity ratio of 0.50 only results if leases and financing obligations are counted. With bonds and loans alone it is 0.29 [own calculation]. The earnings growth of 40.0% per year is the cap the Analyzer displays. Calculated from EPS in 2023 ($2.90) to 2025 ($7.17), it is about 57% per year, also including investment gains. The share count rose 4.6% over three years (shares outstanding per the cover pages of the quarterly reports, October 2023 to October 2026). The tool shows +4.8% and still “STABLE”, because its threshold is ±5%. Amazon has not bought back a single share since 2023; stock-based compensation over the last twelve months was $19.9B.

Equity ratio and P/B also look better than the business alone would justify: the equity of $613.6B includes $102.3B of accumulated other comprehensive income (AOCI), mostly unrealized gains on Anthropic’s convertible notes. These gains do not run through the income statement but go straight into equity.

Two ledgers side by side: on the left, profit rises through book gains on investments; on the right, more cash flows into capex than the business brings in. New debt closes the gap. ↗ Click to enlarge

Cash: Operating cash flow over the last twelve months rose 29% to $168.3B. Capex rose faster, by 82% net to $210.7B. The third quarter alone accounted for $75.9B. The bottom line is free cash flow of −$42.4B, versus +$14.8B a year earlier. In addition, about $60B went into OpenAI and Anthropic preferred stock in 2026. Amazon is sticking to about $220B of capex for 2026. In the first nine months it was $172.2B, so arithmetically about $48B would remain for the fourth quarter, a good third less than in the third. Amazon has not explained this. For 2027, CEO Andy Jassy announces “meaningfully more”.

Balance sheet: At the end of 2025, Amazon had $123.0B in cash and marketable securities against $68.4B of bonds and loans, so more cash than debt. As of September 30, 2026, $114.2B of cash and securities face about $180B of bonds and loans, including the current portion. Net debt is therefore about $66B [own calculation], plus $114.8B of lease liabilities. Long-term debt alone rose from $65.6B to $173.1B since the start of the year. In 2026 Amazon issued bonds in U.S. dollars, euros, Swiss francs, Canadian dollars and British pounds and took out a $17.5B bank term loan. With operating income of over $100B a year, that is manageable. But it changes the profile: within nine months, a company with more cash than debt has become one that finances a good part of its build-out with bonds.

5) Strategic Themes

AWS and AI demand: The cloud is the reason for the capex. AWS reached $46.0B in revenue in the third quarter, an annualized $184B. Andy Jassy, President and CEO, put the backlog at $578B on the analyst call on October 29, 2026, “growing triple digits year-over-year”. In the earnings release he cites projected growth of over 40% for both 2027 and 2028. According to Jassy, revenue commitments for Amazon’s own chips Graviton and Trainium have each increased more than 20-fold year over year. Amazon intends to sell Trainium outside of AWS from late 2027.

Anthropic and OpenAI: Amazon holds stakes in two AI labs that are also major AWS customers. As of September 30, 2026, Amazon held Anthropic preferred stock with a carrying value of $92.5B and Anthropic convertible notes with a fair value of $146.2B, plus OpenAI preferred stock for $50B (in three installments during 2026). Amazon has also committed up to a further $15B in financing to Anthropic. In return, Anthropic has secured up to 5 gigawatts of Trainium capacity and expanded its AWS commitment in the second quarter by more than $100B over ten years. OpenAI expanded its existing $38B AWS commitment by $100B over eight years and has committed to about 2 gigawatts of Trainium capacity.

The AI loop: Amazon provides capital, the labs buy compute, AWS needs data centers. Every new Anthropic funding round at a higher price turns into a book gain at Amazon. ↗ Click to enlarge

For the income statement this means: if Anthropic’s price rises in a new funding round, Amazon books a gain. That, together with the conversion of notes into shares, is where the $16.8B in the first quarter and most of the $53.4B in the second came from. In the first nine months this triggered $15.2B of tax expense, which Amazon attributes primarily to these upward adjustments. On October 26, 2026, Anthropic publicly filed its IPO registration (S-1) with the SEC; a Nasdaq listing is planned. According to Amazon’s quarterly report, the preferred stock and convertible notes would then be converted into non-voting common stock, the notes only up to Amazon’s 12% ownership cap, subject to lock-ups. My assessment: from then on, Amazon’s other income will move with the share price of another company, up and down. Amazon’s percentage stake in Anthropic has not been disclosed.

The capex: Jassy verbatim: “We still expect 2026 cash CapEx of approximately $220 billion, the majority for AWS, and expect to spend meaningfully more in 2027 with a clear line of sight to attractive financial returns.” Management’s justification is the backlog, which Jassy calls “well-diversified”. How much of it comes from Anthropic and OpenAI, Amazon does not disclose. The question for owners is whether the return on these data centers is sufficient, even if part of the demand comes from two customers that Amazon helps finance.

Amazon Leo: The satellite internet service, called Project Kuiper until November 2025, is to launch an initial service in 2026, according to Amazon. There is an agreement with AT&T for business and public-sector customers. In April 2026 Amazon also agreed to acquire satellite operator Globalstar; closing is planned for 2027.

Workforce: In corporate roles, Amazon cut about 14,000 positions in October 2025 and about 16,000 in January 2026. Total headcount still rose 4%.

Legal and regulatory: The FTC antitrust case from 2023 goes to trial before Judge John H. Chun in Seattle on March 29, 2027; the court denied a request for a postponement in October. Since August 31, 2026, the FTC and 22 states have also been suing over hidden surcharges in Amazon’s ad auctions. The Prime case is closed: in September 2025 Amazon paid $2.5B in a settlement, of which $1.0B was a civil penalty and $1.5B refunds to customers. In the EU, the Commission considers AWS a gatekeeper under the Digital Markets Act in a preliminary view from June 25, 2026; no final decision has been made yet. In Germany, the Federal Cartel Office (Bundeskartellamt) prohibited Amazon’s price control mechanisms toward marketplace sellers in February 2026 and initially skimmed off about €59M; the decision is not final.

Outlook: For the fourth quarter Amazon expects revenue of $240–247B (+12% to +16%) and operating income of $28.5–33.5B, compared with $25.0B in the prior-year quarter, which included $2.4B of special charges. Analysts expect 2027 earnings per share of about $10.9 (stockanalysis.com, adjusted according to the source) or $10.7 (Yahoo Finance) [ESTIMATE — analyst forecast]. For 2026 the sources mix figures with and without the Anthropic gains, so I do not cite one here.

The Analyzer rightly refuses to show a fair value here. Earnings that consist 40% of book gains, multiplied by a historical P/E, do not give a fair value, but the line at almost $600 from section 3. Instead of a number, four perspectives on the same price of $259.30 [ESTIMATE — own calculations, not facts]:

  • GAAP P/E of 21: less than half the median since 2016 (48.3) and close to the industry P/E of 20 that the Analyzer compares against. This number describes the book gain, not the business.
  • P/E without investment gains of about 35: still below its own median, but well above the industry P/E. The median also stems from years with much lower margins (2021: 5.3%, 2023: 6.4%). At 13% today, the P/E comes out lower even if the market values Amazon on revenue exactly as before. That is precisely what the P/S ratio shows.
  • Revenue and cash flow: The P/S ratio of 3.5 sits almost exactly on its median of 3.4. Measured by revenue, Amazon costs as much as on average over the last ten years. The P/CF of 16.7 is below its median of 24.6, but operating cash flow currently does not pay for the capex. At −$42.4B there is no free cash flow yield.
  • Looking ahead: Based on the 2027 analyst consensus, the P/E is about 24. The consensus assumes the capex will pay off.

I skip the P/B of 4.6 against a median of 13.6: Amazon’s equity has multiplied since 2016 and today includes $102B of accumulated other comprehensive income, mostly unrealized gains on Anthropic convertible notes. A comparison with the median makes no sense here.

My assessment: Amazon is not as cheap as the P/E of 21 suggests, and not expensive, measured against its own history. The valuation hinges on a single question, namely whether this year’s $220B and the larger amounts in 2027 deliver the returns management is promising. The $578B backlog argues for it. The customers behind it and the debt financing are the points an owner should keep an eye on.

The moat in retail is the logistics network. Warehouses, sortation centers and in-house delivery at Amazon’s scale cannot be rebuilt in a few years, and they carry Prime, the marketplace and advertising at the same time. The more sellers sell through Amazon, the more they pay for fulfillment and ads, and the more choice customers find. Walmart is now growing fast online, but from a smaller base.

In the cloud, AWS is still the largest provider, but it is growing more slowly than its two pursuers and lost three percentage points of market share in one year. Google brings its own AI models and its own chips (TPUs), which it now also sells to others. Amazon relies on Trainium and on its ties to Anthropic and OpenAI. My assessment: the moat is wide, but it is currently being attacked by two very well-capitalized competitors at once, and Amazon is defending it with money that currently does not come from free cash flow.

8) Customer Perspective

In the American Customer Satisfaction Index study (ACSI, January 2026), Amazon scores 82 out of 100, one point less than the year before and above the online-retailer average of 79. At the same time, the legal cases show where there is friction: the Prime settlement with the FTC was about subscriptions signed up without clear consent and hard to cancel; $1.5B is going back to customers as refunds. The new ad-auction lawsuit and the Bundeskartellamt decision concern the other customer group, the sellers on the marketplace. Amazon does not publish how many Prime members it has. [NOT AVAILABLE]

9) Employee Perspective

As of September 30, 2026, Amazon employed 1,638,000 people, 4% more than a year earlier. About 30,000 corporate positions have been cut since October 2025; total headcount still rose. On Glassdoor Amazon rates about 3.5 out of 5, between 3.4 and 3.6 depending on the subpage [snapshot November 2026]. For a company of this size, with jobs ranging from the warehouse floor to chip design, an average says little. 11) Alien Verdict

Measured by margin and operating income, Amazon has never been stronger: the highest operating margin in company history, a cloud growing almost twice as fast as a year ago, a backlog of $578B. If you only look at the business, you see no reason for concern.

The P/E of 21 still tells the wrong story. Without the book gains from Anthropic it is about 35. And while profit rises on paper, more money flows into data centers than the business brings in. Amazon, at the end of 2025 still a company with more cash than debt, now finances a good part of its build-out with bonds in five currencies. Part of the demand meant to justify this capex comes from two AI labs in which Amazon itself holds stakes. Amazon does not disclose how large that part is. This does not have to go wrong. But it is a different company from the one I analyzed a year ago.

The next checkpoints are concrete: whether fourth-quarter capex actually falls to about $48B or the $220B outlook is exceeded, the 2027 number with the annual results in February, whether AWS reaches growth above 40%, at what price Anthropic goes public, and how the antitrust trial starting March 29, 2027 unfolds.

“Amazon has booked the profit from Anthropic. It gets paid only when someone buys the shares. The data centers, on the other hand, are already paid for.”
