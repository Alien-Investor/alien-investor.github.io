Rollins Inc. (ROL)
When AI Picks the Exterminator

by Alien Investor – November 5, 2026, after the third-quarter results (replaces the November 2025 analysis)

On October 21, 2026, Rollins reported its 100th consecutive quarter of revenue growth. The milestone was easy to miss, because the next morning the company behind Orkin cut its growth guidance for the second time in three months. At its low in early October the stock was almost 55% below its record close from February ($65.60); today the gap is 41.5%. There are no fewer pests. What changed, according to management, is something else: how homeowners find an exterminator. This analysis replaces the November 2025 version and sorts out what hits the business and what only hits the valuation.

“Pests don’t disappear because an AI answers. But whoever isn’t in the answer doesn’t get the call.”

Rollins is one of the largest pest control companies in the world, focused on North America. More than 2.8 million residential and commercial customers, about 22,000 employees, more than 850 locations (including franchises). The best-known brand is Orkin, alongside a range of others such as HomeTeam Pest Defense, Fox Pest Control, Clark, Western Pest Services and Critter Control. About three quarters of revenue comes from contracts with fixed, recurring visits.

October 22 stands out. The evening before, the stock had fallen 7.5% after hours; it opened at $31.84 and still closed 5.6% higher. Before the results it had already nearly halved from its record (−49%). Much of the bad news was in the price before it arrived.

Three things came together in 2026:

  • Fewer new residential customers: Residential organic growth fell from 4.2% in the first quarter to 2.3% in the third. Management names AI search as the main reason: language models such as ChatGPT and Gemini recommend a wider range of providers than classic Google search, and fewer leads reach Rollins’ brands.
  • Two guidance cuts: In February the annual report still said 7–8% organic growth for 2026, in July “at least 6%”, in October 4–6%. Rollins will not give a number for 2027 before the full-year results in February.
  • Margins below last year: The operating margin was 20.5% in the third quarter versus 21.9%. Higher insurance and claims costs more than explain the gap, measured against a prior-year quarter with particularly low claims costs.

2) Business Model & Segments

The business is simple and has barely changed in decades. A technician runs a route and visits customers monthly, every other month or quarterly. He treats for insects, rodents or termites and checks bait stations. Most customers pay for ongoing protection, not for a single visit. Rollins puts the mix at year-end 2025 at about 75% recurring services, 10% ancillary services (such as exclusion, insulation, crawlspaces) and 15% one-time services.

Two growth sources come on top of the base. Price: Rollins sees itself as a business that pushes through a bit more than inflation every year, according to CFO Will Harkins 3–4% across the whole portfolio, more in commercial than in residential. Acquisitions: In 2025 there were 26 deals (22 companies and 4 franchise buybacks), including Saela Pest Control for $207.2M. Fox Pest Control was added in 2023 for $350M. Acquisitions are meant to add 2–3% revenue growth every year.

What matters for the current situation is how Rollins wins new customers. Orkin relies heavily on customers who search and call on their own. Fox and HomeTeam, by contrast, sell a lot door to door, and HomeTeam also through home builders who install pest protection during construction. Exactly this difference shows up in the 2026 numbers.

The tool’s return on equity of 36.0% rests on a different equity basis. Using trailing twelve-month earnings and equity at quarter-end it is about 38% [own calculation]. Depending on the count, the share count fell 1.2% (shares outstanding) to 2.4% (weighted diluted shares) over three years; the tool shows −2.3%. The tool’s 481 million shares are an older figure; the cover of the quarterly report lists 478.05 million as of Oct 12, 2026, hence the slightly lower market capitalization in the table above ($18.3B instead of $18.5B). The tool’s 1.91% dividend yield matches the last twelve months of payments; with the new quarterly dividend it is 2.1%.

Margins: In the third quarter the operating margin fell 1.4 percentage points to 20.5%. Insurance and claims together cost 1.6 points, fleet and fuel 0.6. Rollins saved 0.4 points on personnel because it adjusted staffing to lower demand after the weak second quarter. The comparison is distorted: in the third quarter of 2025 insurance and claims were only 1.8% of revenue, versus 2.7% for the first nine months of 2025. Excluding that effect, the incremental margin on new revenue would have been above 25%, the company says. The reported figure is −2.8% (adjusted EBITDA). That is a management adjustment, not an audited number.

Cash and balance sheet: Rollins needs very little capital spending. In the first nine months, $532.7M of operating cash flow faced only $19.9M of capex, leaving $512.8M of free cash flow. Rollins spent $263.1M on dividends, $162.6M on acquisitions and $140.0M on open-market buybacks (3.49 million shares at an average of $40.10, of which $120.0M in the third quarter), more than its free cash flow; short-term borrowings covered the rest. Debt is modest: a $500M bond (5.25%, due 2035) and $239.6M of short-term borrowings, against $129.1M of cash. Rollins puts its adjusted net debt including leases at 1.0 times adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization and rent (end of 2025: 0.9). Solid, but not a fortress balance sheet, and in this business it doesn’t need to be.

On the dividend: On October 20 Rollins raised the quarterly dividend by about 10% to $0.20 (payable Dec 10, 2026). That doubles the regular dividend since early 2022. At today’s price it yields 2.1%.

5) Strategic Themes

AI search: CEO Jerry Gahlhoff laid out the explanation on the analyst call on October 22, 2026. Unlike classic search, Gahlhoff said, language models recommend a wider range of providers that varies by platform, market and even day. Brands that live on customers searching on their own receive fewer leads as a result. Rollins’ response: show price ranges so the models pick them up (done “literally in weeks”, in Gahlhoff’s words), more local content, maintained listings and customer reviews, and first paid ads on ChatGPT and Google Gemini. Explicitly not the plan: simply spending more on marketing.

Context: whether AI search is the main cause cannot be verified from the outside. It is management’s explanation. In July, management had not yet been able to name a cause. The largest competitor, Rentokil (Terminix), also grew only weakly in North America in the same quarter, but says AI search accounts for only a very small share of its leads.

The guidance path: At its investor day in May (release dated May 14, 2026) Rollins laid out its medium-term targets: 7–8% organic growth, 2–3% from acquisitions, an incremental margin of about 30–35% on each additional revenue dollar (adjusted EBITDA) and free cash flow above net income. Ten weeks later came the first cut for 2026, three months after that the second. For 2026 the guidance is now 4–6% organic, 2–3% from acquisitions, incremental margin “slightly positive to 10%”. CFO Harkins still sees a path back to 7–8%, but will only discuss 2027 with the full-year results in February.

Leadership: William Harkins has been CFO since June 15, 2026. He joined in March 2025 as chief accounting officer and succeeded Kenneth Krause. Gahlhoff left no doubt about the situation: “Our third quarter performance did not meet our expectations, and we are not satisfied with these results.”

The Rollins family: A group around the founding family held 37.9% of the shares in September 2026, according to its SEC filing. In November 2025 two family companies (LOR, Inc. and Rollins Holding Company) sold 20 million shares at $57.50, about $1.15B. Rollins itself bought back 3.48 million of those shares for about $200M. On October 29, 2026, LOR bought 1,444,402 shares in the market at an average of $34.59, about $50M. LOR and the R. Randall Rollins Voting Trust each report this purchase on separate forms; it is one purchase, not two. The lock-up from the 2025 sale expires around November 10, 2026.

Non-competes: On June 22, 2026, the US Federal Trade Commission finalized a consent order: Rollins may no longer enforce non-competes against more than 18,000 current and former employees. There was no fine. Gahlhoff calls the topic “pretty much a non-issue”.

Outlook (analyst consensus, stockanalysis.com, as of Oct 23, 2026): Revenue 2026E $4.05B, 2027E $4.35B. Earnings per share 2026E $1.14, 2027E $1.23 [ESTIMATE — analyst forecast, adjusted per the source]. The consensus rating is “hold”.

This is the core of the analysis. The Analyzer reads “35% below fair value”. The math behind it is simple: current earnings per share ($1.09) times the median P/E ratio of the last ten years (53.9) gives about $58.75. That is a model calculation, not a price target, and it rests on one decisive assumption: that the market will again pay 54 times earnings.

Rollins earned that multiple in a particular phase: double-digit revenue growth (2021–2025: 10–14% a year), recent organic growth of about 7% (2025: 6.9%) and a reliability that is rare on the stock market. Exactly that assumption cracked in 2026. A median from the past says nothing about whether the reasons for the premium still hold. I therefore do not take the indicator as a signal.

Three angles on the same $38.38 price [ESTIMATE — own calculations, not facts]:

  • Against its own history: P/E 35.2 instead of 53.9. In the whole period since 2016, Rollins has only been valued this low in the last few months.
  • Against the market: The sector P/E the Analyzer compares against is 20. Rollins still costs about 75% more. The PEG ratio of 2.65 says the same: for the earnings growth of the last three years (13.3% a year) the stock is not cheap.
  • Looking ahead: On adjusted consensus 2027 earnings of $1.23 the P/E is about 31. The free cash flow yield is about 3.7%, or 3.3% without the tax refund.

My take: the market has removed a large part of the quality premium, but not all of it. With 4–6% organic growth, a P/E of 35 is still a bet that Rollins finds its way back to the old growth rates. If it does, the valuation is low by historical standards. If it doesn’t, even a P/E of 30 is no bargain.

The moat as a loop: more customers per route lower the cost per visit, the higher margin funds acquisitions that densify the network. Three inflows drive it, and exactly one of them thinned in 2026. ↗ Click to enlarge

The moat has two sides. The cost side is route density. A technician with five customers on one street instead of one drives less and fits in more visits per day. A local one-man operation cannot copy that advantage, and Rollins widens it with every acquisition. Nothing changed on this side in 2026.

The demand side is the brand. Anyone who saw “Orkin” on vans and in search results for decades called Orkin. This is exactly where AI search hits. When a language model answers a question about an exterminator with five local firms, the well-known brand counts for less, and reviews, prices and availability count for more. My take: the cost moat stands, the brand moat is getting shallower. How much depends on whether Rollins becomes as visible in the new channels as it used to be on Google. The door-to-door brands show that Rollins can win customers without a search engine.

8) Customer Perspective

Pest control is not something people skip when termites are in the beams or rats in the restaurant kitchen. That supports the recurring contracts and the annual price increases. In October management again called retention “strong”, and the recurring residential customer count kept growing. The problem is winning new customers, not cancellations.

What is new is that customer reviews now directly influence new business: language models also base their recommendations on reviews and listings. Rollins has named this itself as a lever. 9) Employee Perspective

Rollins employed 21,946 people at the end of 2025, most of them as field technicians. The business depends on them: in termite and ancillary, staffing shortages in some markets held back growth in 2026 while the backlog grew. After the weak second quarter Rollins adjusted staffing to lower demand. Since the FTC order in June, Rollins can no longer enforce non-competes against more than 18,000 current and former employees. 11) Alien Verdict

Alien Analyzer V2, Dividends tab (Nov 5, 2026): dividends per share grew 13.1% a year over ten years. With the price drop, the yield (blue line) rose to its highest level in the period. The increase to $0.20 is not yet in the chart. ↗ Click to enlarge

Operationally Rollins is not a turnaround case. Revenue grows for the 100th quarter in a row, commercial and termite keep adding, free cash flow exceeds net income, the dividend rises 10%. The family that sold $1.15B of stock at $57.50 a year ago is now buying about $50M at $34.59.

The situation is still serious, and at a specific point: in residential, almost half of revenue, the business grows only 2.3% organically. Management misjudged twice this year and has no number for 2027 yet. Whether AI search is a transition that Rollins absorbs within a year or two through price transparency, reviews and new ad channels, or whether it permanently erodes Orkin’s brand advantage, nobody can say today. Rollins can’t either.

The price has absorbed much of this. Measured against its own history the stock is as cheap as it has been in a long time, measured against today’s growth it is not cheap. The Analyzer’s “35% below fair value” assumes a premium that Rollins has to earn back first. The next checkpoints are concrete: lead volumes in the fourth quarter, the 2027 guidance in February, margins once the prior-year claims effect runs out, and whether the family keeps buying or sells again after the lock-up ends around November 10.

“A moat made of vans and routes can’t be googled. A moat made of name recognition can, and that is the one being re-surveyed right now.”
