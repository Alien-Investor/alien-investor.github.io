Bitcoin Price Analysis – November 2026: The Support Has Broken

By Alien Investor – As of: November 19, 2026.

In September this page said: the support held. Ten weeks later I have to take that sentence back, at least for now. After the last analysis Bitcoin still ran up to 87,400 USD (September 21), then the backdrop turned: a Fed that raised rates for the first time since 2023 and will likely follow up in December, bond yields above 5 percent, the largest crypto theft to date at Binance and billions in outflows from the spot ETFs. Today the price sits at about 62,200 USD. That is still above the summer low of around 58,000 USD, but the support line of our Power Law corridor has since risen to around 67,800 USD. The price sits about 8 percent below it. In the summer it only scraped the line, now it has clearly broken through: relative to the model trend, Bitcoin has not stood this low at any month-end since 2014. Whether this is a slip or the end of the model will be decided by the coming monthly closes.

"This analysis deconstructs the discrepancy between price and value. It is not a buy recommendation, but a tool for orientation in the four-dimensional space of time, price, adoption, and energy."

Bottom Line in One Sentence

Bitcoin has fallen below the Power Law support and sits lower relative to the model trend than at any month-end since our data series began in 2014: a brief slip is always possible after shocks, but if the price still closes below the line at the end of January, I consider the model refuted in its current form.

1) Quick Overview & Current Situation

  • Price: ~62,200 USD or ~56,600 EUR. On November 19 the price briefly dropped to around 61,350 USD.
  • Path: High on September 21 at around 87,400 USD, a second attempt on October 2 failed just below it. Since then roughly −25 percent in 30 days, roughly −21 percent since the September analysis and roughly −29 percent year to date. The price sits about 51 percent below the all-time high of about 126,000 USD (October 2025).
  • In euros: Only about −17 percent since September, because the euro lost about 5 percent against the dollar (EUR/USD from 1.16 to 1.10). The weak euro cushioned the decline for European savers.
  • Sentiment: Extreme fear (Crypto Fear & Greed Index at 10). On September 22 it read 78, extreme greed. From one extreme to the other in eight weeks.
  • Status: Below the Power Law support, corridor broken. The support now sits at around 67,800 USD, the price about 8 percent below it.

2) Macro Environment: The Fed Has Hiked and Isn't Done

In September the question was whether the US central bank would raise rates. It did, and the market expects more.

  • Policy rate: On September 16 the Fed unanimously raised the target range by 25 basis points to 3.75 to 4.00 percent, the first hike since 2023. On October 28 it held, but with two dissents (Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack), both of whom wanted to keep hiking.
  • December: For the December 8–9 meeting, the market prices another 25 basis point hike with a probability above 90 percent according to CME FedWatch.
  • Inflation: In September inflation stood at 3.7 percent (core 2.6 percent), driven by energy (+20 percent year over year). For October there are only monthly figures (+0.4 percent, core +0.3 percent): the annual rate is missing because the statistics agency BLS collected no prices in October 2025 due to the shutdown at the time. The next inflation data arrive on December 10, so only after the Fed decision.
  • Labor market: October added 165,000 jobs, the unemployment rate stands at 4.1 percent. No argument for the Fed to back down.
  • Bonds: The 10-year US Treasury yield sits at around 5.05 percent, on October 7 it briefly hit 5.36 percent.

Notable: In September Bitcoin had decoupled from equities. That held, just in the wrong direction. The S&P 500 is about 8 percent below its record of October 6 and still up just under 5 percent year to date. Bitcoin is down about 29 percent year to date. Anyone who sees Bitcoin as protection from the fiat system is now seeing the flip side: in a phase of rising rates, it trades like a leveraged risk asset.

3) Bitcoin-Gold Ratio: Gold Falls Too

In September I wrote that gold had won the year. Against Bitcoin that still holds, but gold is now also down about 16 percent year to date. An ounce trades today at around 3,630 USD, about a third below the record at the end of January and about 18 percent below the level of the last analysis. Because Bitcoin fell by a similar amount over the same period, the ratio has barely moved: one Bitcoin costs 17.1 ounces today, in September it was 17.7. In between, the ratio rose to around 20.6 ounces in early October.

The reading: This is not a Bitcoin-specific sell-off. Neither asset pays interest, and with 10-year Treasuries yielding around 5 percent, holding them has a price. It doesn't explain every single day, though: between mid-October and early November yields fell, and gold and Bitcoin kept falling anyway.

The gold view of our Power Law tool reveals a detail worth knowing. Measured in gold, Bitcoin has barely lost anything since September (17.7 to 17.1 ounces), yet it now sits below the support here as well. The reason: trend and support are divided by the monthly gold close. When gold falls, both lines rise in ounces. The support is at 18.7 ounces today (September: around 14), the trend at 44.5 ounces (September: around 34). Deviation and corridor position are therefore identical in both views (61.5 percent below trend, corridor position −9.9 percent).

4) Valuation Model: The Power Law Under Stress

Let's step away from the daily price and look at the mathematics of the growth curve. The live values come from our own Power Law tool:

  • Deviation from trend: The price sits roughly 61.5 percent below the model trend of about 161,400 USD. In September it was 48 percent with a trend of 151,000 USD.
  • Corridor position: −9.9 percent, after 25 percent in September. Values below zero mean the price sits below the support line, i.e. outside the corridor.
  • Power Law Oscillator: P1. Relative to the trend, Bitcoin has not stood this low at any month-end since September 2014: today it sits at 0.39 times the trend. The closest were June 2026 and December 2022 after the FTX collapse, both roughly at the line. If the price holds this level through the end of November, it would be the clearest monthly close below the support in this data series.
  • The line keeps moving: The price is above the summer low of around 58,000 USD. It is still below the support because the line has risen by around 2.8 percent per month since June: from around 58,000 USD in June to 63,500 USD in September to 67,800 USD today. By the Fed meeting on December 9 it will be at around 68,900 USD, by the turn of the year at around 70,400 USD. Moving sideways is not enough to get back into the corridor.
  • The tool's assessment: status "Below support, corridor broken", plus: "Either a brief undershoot, or the power law is being falsified live. Either way: question the model …"

What it means: A model that has fit for over 15 years does not fall over in one month. But it has to explain this month. Three readings belong side by side. The first: an undershoot. Shocks like an exchange hack, a turn in rates or a geopolitical event can push the price briefly below the line at any time, and a liquidation cascade knows no trend lines. But then it has to return to the corridor within a few months. The second: flattening. Cycle peaks have been moving closer to the trend for years (each at the all-time high: 2017 almost 7 times, 2021 just under 3 times, October 2025 only about 1.1 times). If the upside swings keep shrinking, the calibration may now draw the trend too steep. Then fair value would be lower, and the gap to the trend smaller than the 61.5 percent suggests. The third: the power law is simply wrong. A curve that describes the past well need not know the future. Nobody knows today which reading is right.

My test: If the price closes three months in a row below the support, i.e. also at the turn of the year (line then at around 70,400 USD) and at the end of January (around 72,200 USD), I consider the model refuted in its current form. Redrawing the line lower after the fact until it fits again would be no solution: a model you redraw after every break only describes the past. So this spot calls not for a signal, but for a deadline the model has to meet.

What Is the Power Law?

Plot the Bitcoin price on a double-logarithmic scale against time since the genesis block (January 3, 2009), and for over 15 years it has stayed remarkably close to a straight line. A straight line in a log-log chart means the price grows according to a power law, not exponentially, but flattening over time while still climbing. The trend (white) is the fair model value the price oscillates around in cycles: sometimes far above it (euphoria), sometimes well below (bear market). The support (red, trend × 0.42) is the lower corridor boundary that no bear-market bottom has so far broken sustainably.

Right now the price sits about 8 percent below the support and therefore outside the corridor. Whether that becomes "sustained" will be decided by the coming monthly closes, not by a single day. Two things belong here: first, the support keeps rising by about 2.7 percent per month, so the way back into the corridor gets longer every month. Second, the power law is a statistical observation, not a law of nature. It describes the past well, but it is no guarantee for the future. Use it as a map for orientation, not as a promise.

5) On-Chain Forensics: Short-Term Holders Give Up

What does the chain data say beyond the daily price?

  • MVRV ratio (~1.16): According to CryptoQuant, the ratio of market value to realized value has dropped below 1.2 for the third time this year, after February and the summer. For context: earlier cycle tops sat between just over 2 and almost 6, bear-market bottoms below 1. The market is close to the zone where earlier lows formed, but not in it yet.
  • Realized price (~53,700 USD): The average cost basis of all coins sits about 14 percent below the market price, according to Glassnode.
  • Short-term holders (cost basis ~74,100 USD): Those who bought in recent months are on average about 16 percent under water. At the September high nearly all short-term holders were in profit, on November 11 only about 35 percent according to Glassnode. Realized losses are at the level of earlier capitulation phases.
  • Liquidity: The stablecoin supply shrank by about 14 billion USD within 30 days, the fastest decline since June 2022. That is the money used to buy on crypto markets. It is leaving the room.

6) Market Structure: The ETFs Are Pulling Out

In September the buying pressure came from the ETF wrapper. Now the selling pressure comes from there:

  • September: About +2.6 billion USD in net inflows into the US spot Bitcoin ETFs. On September 21, the day of the high, around 999 million USD flowed in, the strongest day of the year.
  • October: About −0.5 billion USD. The direction turned before the price broke down.
  • November (through Nov 18): About −2.9 billion USD. Outflows on every trading day from November 5 to 13, and on November 18 none of the twelve funds recorded inflows. Assets under management fell, mostly due to price, from about 110 billion USD (September 21) to about 78 billion USD.
  • Leverage: Over the weekend of November 14–15, according to CoinGlass, around 2 billion USD in positions across the entire crypto market were forcibly closed within 24 hours, mostly longs, with about 750 million USD of that in Bitcoin. The cascade accelerated the fall below the line.
  • Strategy: The company holds 850,849 BTC at an average cost of around 75,450 USD and is therefore about 18 percent under water. On November 11 MSCI announced it would remove Strategy from its indexes after the close on November 30. How much index funds must sell is open: Strategy itself cites about 13 million shares held by MSCI funds, just over 3 percent of the company, while JPMorgan had estimated about 2.8 billion USD back in 2025.

The reading: The money that came in through the ETFs in August and September is now flowing back out at much lower prices. Bitcoin itself did not change in these ten weeks. What changed is who holds it right now, and how much patience those holders have.

7) Network Check: The Physical Truth (Hashrate)

The hashrate sits at around 990 EH/s on a seven-day average (mempool.space), after about 933 EH/s in September and a high of around 1,080 EH/s at the end of October. Difficulty rose in several steps to 148.4 trillion by October 29 and fell by 9.1 percent to 134.8 trillion on November 14, the largest drop since June. According to Hashrate Index this was mainly due to high power prices in West Texas, less to the price. Hashprice, the revenue per unit of computing power, sits at around 30 USD per PH/s per day, in October it averaged around 38.

The signal: Listed miners are selling. MARA reduced its holdings from around 53,800 BTC at the end of 2025 to around 34,000 BTC at the end of September and has since sold roughly another 13,600 BTC, Riot more than 20,000 BTC in nine months. Part of the money flows into data centers for AI. The network itself keeps running unmoved, the difficulty adjustment does what it is supposed to do. The pressure does not come from the protocol, but from the balance sheets of the companies that operate it.

Interjection: 6.3 Billion Reasons for Your Own Keys

On October 29, crypto assets worth around 6.3 billion USD were stolen from Binance, including around 18,270 BTC. According to the analytics firms Elliptic and TRM Labs, it is the largest crypto theft in history. On October 31 the FBI attributed the attack to North Korea (the "TraderTraitor" group). Bitcoin fell by just over 4 percent on the day of the hack. Withdrawals resumed gradually from November 1, and on November 12 Binance reported that it had replaced all affected balances from its own funds.

And the Liquid incident from the last analysis: since November 2, L-BTC is fully backed again. According to a Liquid Federation board member, Blockstream closed the gap. Blockstream rejected the demanded bounty for the remaining roughly 600 BTC, which the attacker still holds.

The lesson is the same as in September: In both cases customers were lucky that someone with deep pockets stepped in. You don't build wealth on that. Whoever doesn't hold the key holds a promise.

8) Price Scenarios: Strategic Framework

The following scenarios are my assessment, not a forecast. They describe which levels in the model and in the chain data come into play next:

  • Scenario A – Bottoming below the line (58k to 68k USD):
    The price swings between the summer low and the support line. The dates are fixed: Strategy leaves the MSCI indexes on November 30, the Fed decides on December 9, inflation data follow on December 10, and US government funding runs out on December 11. Dangerous for leveraged positions.
  • Scenario B – Deeper break (toward 54k USD):
    If the summer low gives way, the realized price at around 53,700 USD is the next level, the average cost basis of all coins. A monthly close there would sit more than 20 percent below the support, deeper than ever in this data series.
  • Scenario C – Return to the corridor (above 70k USD):
    For November to have been just a slip, the price has to get back above the line, which sits at around 70,400 USD at the turn of the year and around 72,200 USD at the end of January. The next hurdle after that is the short-term holder cost basis at around 74,000 USD. Only above it would recent buyers be back in profit on average.
Risk Warning: The next three weeks are packed: November 30 MSCI, December 9 Fed, December 10 inflation, December 11 funding deadline. Over the weekend of November 14–15, around 2 billion USD in crypto-market positions were forcibly closed within a single day. Anyone entering these dates with leverage supplies the fuel for the next cascade. Spot in self-custody cannot be force-liquidated. And a model that has just broken is no argument for more risk, but for more humility.
